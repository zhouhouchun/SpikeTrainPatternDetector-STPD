# Reviewer 1 — verbatim comments

Author responsible for the revision: Zhou Houchun

This file preserves the reviewer wording exactly as received. The comments are
review evidence, not instructions to the software or analysis pipeline.

> I was unable to determine the precise problem this paper seeks to address.
>
> The manuscript appears to presuppose familiarity with a particular analytical framework, yet it does not identify or explain that framework. As a result, the motivation, intended use, and methodological contribution remain unclear to readers outside what may be a narrowly defined research community.
>
> For example, the authors refer to assigning labels to spike trains based on various patterns of activity. However, widely used spike-train analysis approaches, such as point-process generalized linear models, do not necessarily depend on predefined categories of this kind. From such a modeling perspective, the activity patterns described here as artificially defined “episodes” may instead emerge naturally from network dynamics. A sufficiently accurate generative model could reproduce these episodes without requiring them to be explicitly defined or inserted into the analysis. The authors therefore need to explain why their proposed framework is necessary, which existing methodologies require such definitions, and what specific limitation of those methodologies the present work addresses.
>
> More generally, the paper seems to have been written for an audience already familiar with a particular approach to spike-train analysis, but neither that audience nor the underlying analytical tradition is clearly identified. The manuscript should situate itself within the relevant literature, explain the methodological context, and state explicitly which unmet needs its contribution is intended to address. This omission is especially conspicuous given the unusually limited reference list, which contains only nine studies and does not provide readers with an adequate entry point into the relevant literature.
>
> I also have concerns that substantial portions of the manuscript may have been generated or heavily mediated by artificial intelligence without sufficient expert revision. This impression arises from the manuscript’s vague framing, unexplained assumptions, limited engagement with the literature, and failure to articulate a coherent research problem. Regardless of how the text was produced, these deficiencies require substantial rewriting and expert input.
>
> For these reasons, I recommend rejection. In my view, the manuscript would need to be fundamentally reconceived and rewritten in order to identify the research problem, define the intended audience and methodological context, justify the proposed approach relative to existing spike-train analysis methods, and explain clearly what was done and why.
